Songs are a wonderful reason to learn a language and a frustrating way to do it. A line moves past before I can separate the words. A translation gives me meaning but not sound. A dictionary gives me a word but removes it from the melody that made me care.

I built Thai Song Learner around a smaller promise: choose a song, then learn one line you can genuinely sing in five minutes.

## Keep the context connected

The useful unit is not only the song, line, or word. It is the movement between them. The interface keeps Thai script, romanization, meaning, and pronunciation close enough that exploration does not become tab management.

Practice mode is for careful reading. KTV mode returns the words to their musical context. Vocabulary quizzes and frequency views reveal what repeats across songs, while memory lessons connect common words into stories.

## Motivation is part of the system

Language tools often optimize an abstract curriculum. A favorite song supplies something equally important: the desire to come back. That changes the product question from “What should a beginner learn next?” to “How can this thing they already love carry more learning?”

The project is still an experiment. I am interested in how much vocabulary a small song library can unlock, where pronunciation support is most useful, and whether the path from listening to singing can feel playful without becoming shallow.
